Last.fm Lens

A magnifier for tidying up scrobbles: duplicate artists, combined artist names, inconsistent track titles, missing or clashing albums, duplicate scrobbles and long-tail oddities. It edits nothing: it tells you what to look at, with the evidence in plain sight.

Drop your scrobble CSV here

The one from lastfmstats.com (lastfmstats-yourname.csv) works, as does any CSV with artist, album, track and date columns.

Everything is processed in your browser. The file is never uploaded anywhere.

How it works, and where its limits lie

Every finding is a scored suspicion from 0 to 100, with the clues that fired and the concrete evidence. The engine proposes; you decide. Nothing in this report asserts that something is definitely wrong.

It is all code

There is no language model and no external service in the analysis: only deterministic functions (normalised keys, sets of tracks, counts and a bounded edit distance) in src/engine.js. The same CSV always produces exactly the same report, and every rule can be read and changed.

Evidence rule

  • Sharing a track is required. An artist duplicate is only proposed when the two names share at least one track, or when the names are identical bar capitalisation, accents or punctuation. Looking similar on its own (starting with “Franc…”, say) never generates a finding.
  • Weak pairs are omitted and counted in the summary, so you always know what was left out. They can be brought back with minimumConfidence: 'low'. Findings folded into a stronger one are counted in the summary too, so nothing disappears quietly.

What each section looks at

  • Duplicate artists: two names pointing at the same artist. The strong proof is sharing tracks: if every track under “Ana” also sits under “Ana Beltrán”, it is almost certainly the same entry. It also counts whether their scrobbles mix within the same sitting (less than ten minutes apart).
  • Combined artists: names such as “Ana, Bruno Salas y Cata”. These are flagged only when the first artist of the name exists separately in your library, and the proposal is exactly that first one (“Ana”). If the first one does not exist, the first that does is used, with a warning that it may not be the main artist. The same name written without the separator (“Ana Bruno”) is swept into the same finding, because it is the same artist spelled differently.
  • Duplicates never merge into a combined name. If the most frequent form of a pair is itself a combined name, nothing is merged into it: everything is renamed straight to the artist it stands for. That is why “Ana Bruno” points at “Ana” and never at “Ana, Bruno”, and why a name already covered by a combined-name finding produces only one card rather than two saying the same thing.
  • Inconsistent track titles: the same track written in several ways under the same artist (capitalisation, “(X)” versus “- X”, with or without a feat. credit).
  • Albums: albums missing on some scrobbles but present on others, the same title split across several albums (single versus album), and albums spelled in several ways.
  • Duplicate scrobbles: the same track twice in a row less than a minute apart.
  • Long tail: artists with one or two scrobbles, likely typos, garbled characters or odd spacing.

What it does NOT do

  • It does not edit your CSV and it does not touch your Last.fm account.
  • It does not decide for you: where two readings are possible, the finding says so explicitly.
  • It does not know any artist’s real discography. “They share 15 tracks” is very good evidence, but it is still an inference from your own data.

The “good” value when there are two candidates

  • By default the proposal is the name that combines being the most recent, the most frequent and the most complete, and the card shows all three so you can disagree with it. If one of the three points the other way, a warning appears.
  • The rule can be changed in src/engine.js (RULES.canonical: recent, frequent or score).

Making the changes in Last.fm

  • Edits to artist, track and album are retroactive: when you edit one scrobble, Last.fm offers to apply it to every scrobble of that combination (and renaming an artist affects the whole entry). It is a bulk change: look twice.
  • Deleting a duplicate scrobble is one at a time, from the scrobble’s (⋯) menu.
  • Mark items “Accept” or “Discard” here and the actions CSV downloads with your decisions applied.

Privacy

  • The analysis runs entirely in your browser. No server, no upload, no network requests.
